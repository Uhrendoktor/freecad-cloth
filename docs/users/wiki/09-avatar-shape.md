## Prepare a mannequin for sewing

Start from any document and select **Create Avatar** from **Cloth Simulation → Avatar & Fitting** or **Cloth Sewing → Fitting & Avatar**. The command adds the built-in Anny-backed mannequin to the active document (and creates a document when none is open). Imported FreeCAD geometry remains a surface provider rather than a deformable human model.

1. Choose **Avatar & Fitting → Edit Avatar** in Cloth Simulation or **Fitting & Avatar → Edit Avatar** in Cloth Sewing.
2. In **Body shape**, adjust **Gender blend** from feminine through neutral to masculine. This is a normalized body-proportion control, not a statement about a person's gender identity. Adjust **Breast fullness** separately to add front projection.
3. Keep **3D Dimensions** enabled. In the viewport, identify the labelled handles for height, shoulder breadth, chest, waist, and hip. The chest, waist, and hip rings indicate circumference; drag the corresponding handle to stage a new measurement.
4. Use **Body measurements** and **Proportions** for precise values, including stomach circumference and sleeve length.
5. Expand **Advanced Anny parameters** for the complete phenotype and local body-morph controls. Search by the original Anny parameter name, then edit its numeric value. Phenotypes are normalized to **0–1**; local body morphs use **−1–1**. **Reset advanced** clears those overrides.
6. Treat advanced values as direct shape overrides: they are applied after anthropometric fitting and can intentionally move the resulting body away from one or more requested measurements. Leave the override at its displayed default to keep measurement fitting in control.
7. Review all staged values, then select **Apply & Rebuild**. The mannequin mesh and its derived fitting landmarks are regenerated using the committed values. Choose **Cancel** instead when the staged edits should be discarded.

## Verify the fit inputs

After rebuilding, confirm that the document's avatar properties contain the intended shape and dimensions. Save the document, close and reopen it, and verify the mannequin again before using it for fitting.

Use at least the front, rear, and side views. Check that the silhouette reflects the selected shape, the height is correct, and the named chest/bust, underbust, waist, and hip measurements match the intended sewing size. Breast fullness and bust circumference are different controls: use the circumference for garment sizing and the shape control for additional projection.

For a small end-to-end exercise, follow [Example 3: Prepare a mannequin for sewing](../EXAMPLES.md#3-prepare-a-mannequin-for-sewing), then continue to [Direct fitting and arrangement](03-fitting.md) to position garment pieces on the target.

## How the viewport editor behaves

The labelled lines, markers, and circumference rings are transient viewport overlay geometry. They do not add FreeCAD document objects. A drag updates the staged task-panel value; hovered handles show the exact millimetre readout while idle labels stay compact. **Apply & Rebuild** is the commit boundary. Closing the editor removes the overlay and its callbacks. The 3D Pattern Pen remains a separate authoring tool and is not reused by this editor.

If a build cannot initialise the viewport interaction, use the numeric measurement fields. Imported FreeCAD geometry is a surface provider rather than a parametric human body, so its shape and measurement controls are disabled.


## Model backend and measurement coverage

The built-in mannequin is generated by [Anny](https://github.com/naver/anny), an Apache-2.0 parametric human-body model, and fitted using [clad-body](https://github.com/datar-psa/clad-body), an Apache-2.0 anthropometric measurement library. Shape fitting optimizes Anny phenotype and local morph parameters against the library's differentiable measurement functions. Cloth then applies its existing MakeHuman-compatible rig and weighted posing pipeline; it no longer uses its custom torso contour-correction pass as the production shape engine.

Cloth's tape-style circumference and length inputs use millimetres; clad-body's differentiable targets use centimetres. The fitting objective targets height, chest/bust, underbust, waist, hip, thigh, knee, calf, upper arm, shoulder width, sleeve length, inseam, and neck. Stomach circumference remains an editable, saved millimetre reference instead of a direct optimization target: CLAD's stomach metric uses a sign-only global gradient in this fit path, and including it made the Anny optimizer produce non-finite vertices. The **Body composition** group also offers an optional target mass in kilograms; when enabled, the fit uses clad-body's differentiable mass measurement to adjust Anny's global weight phenotype.

Other useful garment dimensions—high hip, wrist, elbow, ankle, front/back waist lengths, torso length, crotch length, front/back rise, shirt length, and back-neck-to-waist—are exposed as millimetre fields and saved with the mannequin. They are reference inputs and can inform derived fitting landmarks or downstream garment work, but the current surface-fitting objective does not independently constrain all of them. Volume, BMI, body-fat percentage, and belly-depth are derived clad-body metrics rather than independent shape sliders.

The advanced editor discovers all normalized phenotype parameters and paired local-morph parameters available from the installed Anny assets. Phenotypes use 0–1; local morphs use −1–1. These direct shape overrides are applied after anthropometric fitting, so they can intentionally move the result away from its requested measurements. Reset an override to its default when the standard measurement fit should remain authoritative. Bust/underbust targets and the primary breast-fullness control remain separate.

Anny 0.6.x and clad-body 0.6.x are declared as required project dependencies in `pyproject.toml`; the built-in mannequin is available from the workbench UI without opening a pre-populated document. Anny's first model initialization builds cached model data; later rebuilds reuse those library assets. The integration uses Anny's MakeHuman-compatible rig/topology rather than a non-commercial SMPL/SMPL-X topology.

When the mesh is rebuilt, fitting anchors are refreshed against the stable avatar mesh where possible. If an anchor is visibly marked stale or no longer follows the intended surface, recreate it before arranging fabric. Always review the actual garment-to-target relationship before running simulation.
